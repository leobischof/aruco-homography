Photograph an object next to a printed sheet of ArUco markers. Get back a PDF that you print at 1:1, glue onto wood, and cut out.

The markers are the ruler. Their printed size is known — you measure it with a caliper rather than assume it — so from their corners in the photo the app computes the homography that maps the photographed plane back to a flat, square-on view at a known scale. What comes out is not a nice picture of your object. It is your object, at its true size, on paper.

MILLIMETRES ARE THE PRODUCT

This app is built around one rule: a change that makes the image prettier and moves an edge by half a millimetre is a regression. The image adjustment sliders — greyscale, local contrast, edge lift, brightness — deliberately cannot move a single millimetre. They change what you see, never what you cut.

EVERYTHING HAPPENS ON YOUR PHONE

There is no server, no account and no upload. The app does not even hold the permission to reach the network, so it cannot send anything — that is enforced by Android, not promised by the developer. The whole chain, from marker detection to the finished PDF, runs in a native core on your device.

WHAT IT DOES

* Finds the markers in the live camera image and tells you when the plane is good enough to shoot
* Warns you instead of drawing a grid it does not believe
* Corrects for object thickness, so a sheet lying on a 12 mm board still measures right
* Lets you set the crop in millimetres and trace an outline
* Exports a tiled template across A4 or Letter pages, with overlap, a glue plan and labelled grid lines
* Prints a 100 mm control scale on every sheet, so you can prove your printer did not scale

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO

Lens distortion is not corrected — use the telephoto lens and stand back. Curved objects are impossible in principle: a homography describes exactly one plane. And the app cannot check your printer for you, which is why the control scale is there.

BEFORE YOU CUT

Measure the finished piece. The app is honest about what it has proven: the chain from PDF to printer to paper has been verified with a caliper. The chain from photo to markers to millimetres has not yet been independently confirmed against an object of known length. Treat the first piece as a test.

You will need the marker sheet, which the app generates for you to print, and a caliper to measure it with.
